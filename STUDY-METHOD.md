# How this app supports grammar learning

Reviewed 3 November 2026. Research supports the principles below, not this app
as a tested intervention. No method guarantees N2 success. These papers do not
establish one ideal schedule for every learner or for every grammar pattern.

## What to do

1. **Understand:** read the short meaning and key clue, then an example. Check
   the grammar notes for attachment rules and restrictions when needed.
2. **Try:** choose an answer before opening an explanation. Read the feedback,
   especially the distinction behind a wrong answer. Using help is learning.
3. **Return:** do due reviews on later days. New examples and mixed patterns
   help check whether you recognize the use beyond the example you just read.
4. **Use it:** optionally say a sentence about your own life. Ask a teacher or
   proficient speaker for feedback. This app cannot assess free speech.

## Evidence and implementation

| Principle | Research | What the app does; limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spaced practice | Kim & Webb (2022), a meta-analysis of second-language learning, found benefits of spacing, with effects depending on the task and retention interval. | Due reviews return across study days. Success before a due date does not advance the scheduled level. The particular 1, 3, 7 and 21-day intervals are practical defaults, not an experimentally optimized schedule. |
| Attempt retrieval before checking | Roediger & Karpicke (2006) found better delayed retention from testing than repeated study of prose passages. | Questions hide the answer until an attempt or reveal; phrase cards offer Hide & say it. The prose-memory experiment does not establish Japanese grammar transfer. Multiple choice tests recognition; it is not equivalent to producing a sentence unaided. |
| Explicit grammar explanation | Spada & Tomita (2010) found advantages for explicit instruction across simple and complex English grammar features in their meta-analysis. | Meanings, use cues, comparisons and form notes remain available. A memorable phrase is an entry point, not a replacement for understanding the rule. The findings do not test this app or Japanese N2 specifically. |
| Corrective feedback | Butler & Roediger (2008) found that feedback improved the benefits and reduced the misleading effects of multiple-choice testing. | Feedback follows each answer, with the correct answer and an explanation; authored questions explain distractors. This supports giving corrective feedback, not claiming immediate feedback is always superior to delayed feedback. |
| Mixed grammar practice | Nakata & Suzuki (2019) found better one-week retention from interleaved than blocked practice of five English structures, despite more errors during practice. | Daily reviews mix due patterns and rotate available questions. The first lesson and its initial check stay together as an introduction. Short, targeted practice is also available. The study does not establish an ideal mix for every N2 learner. |

## How progress is counted

- A new lesson's immediate check is introductory practice. It cannot increase
  its review level as evidence of remembering on a later day.
- A wrong answer returns the daily pattern to early review. A correct answer
  after opening a relevant lesson, looking back at its answer, or using a hint
  does not earn an unaided promotion. Same-day retries do not earn another step.
- Correct optional practice before a due date leaves that review date and level
  unchanged. An incorrect or assisted attempt can bring a review forward.
- The targeted exercise tracker also requires due, unaided success on separate
  study days to advance. Its records are separate from the daily review ladder,
  but due saved exercises return automatically through daily Study on later
  days. The level filter, daily cap and one-exercise-per-pattern limit apply.
- Random JLPT drills save attempts and feedback separately. Their scores do not
  promote the daily review level or estimate an exam score.
- Missed or assisted drill questions are scheduled for a later study day.
  New drill rounds prioritize due mistakes while keeping passage blanks together
  and mixing formats. Known sentence-gap mistakes can also enter daily Study,
  within its level filter and review cap. Unstudied targets stay in the drill
  until encountered in Study; order/passage questions return in later drills.
  The next-day delay is a practical default, not a research-derived optimum.
- Daily Study and random drills share question-exposure history. A same-day
  repeat cannot promote the daily review level or clear a pending delayed check.
  Looking up the relevant lesson or revisiting its answer counts as help.
  A correct, unassisted attempt on a later due day clears the question's repair
  flag; it does not certify general mastery of the grammar.
- The 0–100 **practice index** summarizes review level, answer history, lapses
  and lateness. Its weights are a design heuristic. It is neither a probability
  of remembering nor a validated measurement of mastery or exam readiness.
- Where there is no authored question, a reveal-and-compare check relies on
  **your self-assessment**. The app cannot verify what you recalled or said.
  Those answers are not objective evidence of production skill.

## Defaults and content limits

Three new patterns per day, six-step rounds, review caps and the interval ladder
are adjustable or implementation defaults. Research does not establish those
exact values. Choose a new-pattern allowance that leaves time for due reviews;
20 per day is an option, not a recommendation for everyone.

The library contains 467 entries, including support skills. The N2 drill has
146 questions (44 official and 102 original), targeting 123 lesson IDs. The
bank cannot supply a distinct unseen assessment for every lesson. Rotation
reduces repetition where alternatives exist; it cannot remove familiarity
with a small bank or ensure transfer to real conversations. The app has not
undergone an independent linguistic review of every explanation and distractor.

With the N2 Study filter, 310 entries are eligible, including mapped foundations
and support skills. Currently 92 have an eligible sentence-gap drill question,
nine more have an authored targeted exercise, and 209 use reveal-and-compare
self-checks. Some of those also appear in order or passage drills. These counts
describe the app's current routes, not an official exhaustive N2 grammar list.

For exam preparation, complete due Study reviews, then use the mixed drill to
practise choosing grammar under context. Before tapping, consider both meaning
and what attaches immediately before/after the blank. After a mistake, identify
the clue that changes the answer. The precise daily workload should fit your
available time; the app's selectable 20 new patterns is not a universal target.

Grammar practice is only part of N2 preparation. The official test also assesses
vocabulary, reading and listening. Passing requires an overall 90/180 and at
least 19/60 in each scoring section; a high grammar-drill percentage cannot be
converted to those scaled scores. See the official
[test structure](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/testsections.html) and
[passing requirements](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/results.html).

  memory tests improves long-term retention*. Psychological Science, 17, 249–255.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/
- Spada, N., & Tomita, Y. (2010). *Interactions Between Type of Instruction and

- Butler, A. C., & Roediger, H. L. (2008). *Feedback enhances the positive
